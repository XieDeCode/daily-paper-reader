# GOFusion: Real-Time Gaussian-Occupancy Submap Fusion for Fisher-Informed Autonomous Robot Navigation 

Boxin Bu 1,2, Simon Boche 1, Haoming Zhang 2, Jaehyung Jung 1, Jiaxin Wei 1, Hannah Schieber 3,Daniel Roth 3, Angela P. Schoellig 2,4, and Stefan Leutenegger 1

Abstract —In this letter, we present GOFusion , a real-time visual mapping system for autonomous robot navigation that anchors Gaussian primitives to local occupancy maps, providing both spatial awareness for path planning and high-fidelity appear-ance that enables information-driven robot navigation. GOFusion 

builds 3D Gaussian maps from pose estimates and local occu-pancy maps obtained via visual-inertial simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) and enables pose corrections to maintain both geometric and visual consistency. Furthermore, it restricts the 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization to active submaps to bound GPU memory usage. Building on the appearance-rich Gaussian map, we introduce a rasterizer-integrated Fisher approximation that quantifies per-primitive sensitivity online, serving as an information-driven heuristic for Gaussian pruning and next-best-view planning. We evaluate GOFusion on public datasets and in real-world drone flights. It not only outperforms several baselines in mapping quality, but also achieves real-time, high-fidelity and uncertainty-aware mapping at scale. Finally, we demonstrate fully autonomous drone exploration for Gaussian map refinement using the proposed Fisher-informed heuristic. Code is available at https://github.com/Bennnoo/GOFusion. We provide a supplementary video demonstration at https://youtu. be/pLQKbxQvb6I. 

Index Terms —SLAM, Gaussian Splatting, Occupancy Map-ping, Autonomous Robot Navigation 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Autonomous robots need maps that support safe motion, reliable localization, and visual understanding in unknown environments. Occupancy maps [1]–[3] distinguish between free, occupied, and unknown space so that planners can check collisions, extract frontiers [4], and generate paths in large environments [5]. Such maps, however, encode geometry alone and discard appearance, whereas advanced tasks such as inspection and human-robot interaction require detailed scene appearance. Radiance field map representations address this limitation, either by implicitly encoding the scene in a neural network [6] or by explicitly optimizing Gaussian primitives via 3D Gaus-sian Splatting (3DGS) [7]. While the former relies on dense per-ray sampling of an implicit field and therefore remains costly to train and query, 3DGS supports efficient rendering

However, vanilla 3DGS maps describe only observed surfaces and do not distinguish free space from potentially occupied space, which is unsafe for robot navigation. In principle, combining occupancy and Gaussian maps yields a complementary hybrid representation, in which an occupancy layer supports free-space reasoning, completeness assessment, and planning, while a Gaussian layer provides high-fidelity appearance. However, existing systems realize only half of this potential. GSFusion [9] and GPS-SLAM [10] exploit this coupling in one direction, predominantly using the geometric map to improve the reconstruction quality and efficiency rather than to support robot localization and naviga-tion. Therefore, a hybrid representation that directly supports navigation remains an open challenge. To address this challenge, we propose GOFusion , a real-time hybrid Gaussian-and-occupancy mapping system for au-tonomous robot navigation. GOFusion is built directly on a real-time visual-inertial SLAM system, OKVIS2-X [11], and inherits its state estimates and occupancy submaps. However, unlike previous reconstruction-oriented methods, we close the loop between the two map layers. While the occupancy submaps anchor and initialize the Gaussians, photometric optimization of 3DGS, in turn, refines the camera poses, thereby reducing pose jitter and improving map consistency. Our submap-local design keeps online optimization scalable, since trainable states are maintained only for active submaps. Beyond geometry and appearance, we further accumulate Fisher information from the Gaussian primitives to identify poorly-constrained map regions, providing an information-theoretic heuristic for trajectory planning that enables the robot to actively complete its own map. To our knowledge, GOFusion is the first hybrid Gaussian-occupancy mapping system that supports real-time 3D navi-gation outdoors from cameras and an IMU alone. The contri-butions of this paper are: 1) A submap-based 3D Gaussian representation for large-scale high-fidelity mapping that supports appearance-dependent tasks such as inspection and teleoperation alongside navigation, where SLAM pose-graph updates, including loop closures, also directly update both the occupancy and Gaussian layers. 2) A rasterizer-integrated online Fisher approximation that maintains a diagonal, accumulated Fisher information over all Gaussians at negligible cost. Serving as an information heuristic for robot exploration, it highlights another key methodological advantage of Gaussian map-ping in robotics. 3) A real-time navigation framework that integrates visual-inertial SLAM with our hybrid map, deployable either fully onboard or split across onboard and offboard compute for resource-limited platforms. 4) Real-world UAV experiments demonstrate GOFusion ’s online capabilities and how Fisher information can be used in Gaussian pruning and next-best-view selection. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Volumetric Mapping for Robot Navigation 

Volumetric representations have dominated robot mapping for decades. Occupancy maps [1] explicitly distinguish free, occupied, and unknown space, supporting collision checking, frontier extraction [4], and navigation under partial observa-tion [12]. Truncated signed distance function (TSDF)- and signed distance field (SDF)-based systems such as [13, 14] instead target continuous surface reconstruction. They encode distance to the nearest observed surface, so unobserved volume is not separated from free space by the representation itself and must be handled by convention. Octree frameworks such as [3] improve scalability through hierarchical subdivision. These methods scale in space but not in time, since a globally pa-rameterized map must be re-optimized whenever loop closure revises past poses. Submap-based SLAM resolves this issue by integrating measurements into local maps, whose poses are frequently up-dated during graph optimization, improving global map consis-tency across all maps. This concept lends itself to exploration in large environments, where local submaps enable efficient updates while still supporting global planning [5]. OKVIS2-X [11] follows this direction for multi-sensor state estimation with occupancy submapping and submap alignment. Because pose corrections act at the submap level, the occupancy map remains directly available for planning throughout. These advantages, however, concern geometry alone. As occupancy quantifies uncertainty about space, not appearance, a surface can be reliable for collision avoidance yet unusable for inspection or human-robot collaboration. 

B. Gaussian Mapping for Robot Navigation 

A more recent line of work in mapping replaces volu-metric structures with explicit, addable, prunable, and rigidly transformable Gaussian primitives. Moreover, they are also differentiable under a fast rasterizer. Supervision is derived directly from image intensities, so every pixel contributes a residual [7]. Dense visual SLAM systems exploit this to jointly optimize camera poses and Gaussian primitives [15, 16], or to embed Gaussians in online pipelines through selective primitive updates, conventional frontends, and RGB-D align-ment [17]–[19]. Others pair Gaussian maps with dedicated planners or semantic objectives for closed-loop navigation [20, 21]. Hybrid systems go one step further: previous work [9, 10] use TSDF geometry to guide Gaussian insertion and suppress redundant primitives, although the geometry serves reconstruction rather than a trajectory planner. At the robot scale, Gaussian SLAM faces two further obstacles. A single global Gaussian map carries optimizer states, gradients, and rendering buffers that grow with scene size, and, once built, cannot easily adopt pose corrections. LoopSplat [22] addresses the second by organizing Gaussians into submaps that loop closure can rigidly transform, while DiskChunGS [23] addresses the first with disk-backed chunk-ing. Neither, however, maintains an occupancy representation for downstream robotic tasks. 

C. Uncertainty-Aware Mapping for Robot Navigation 

Mapping to support real-world robot navigation, how-ever, requires more than geometry and appearance, since the robot must also decide where to go next. Frontier-based exploration [4, 5] exposes unknown regions, but is blind to observed regions where the appearance model remains poorly constrained. Neural and Gaussian methods therefore study uncertainty-aware view selection, using Fisher infor-mation [24] or related visibility and information-gain sig-nals [25, 26] to improve reconstruction quality. A second line keeps uncertainty inside the estimator, reweighting tracking, registration, and loop constraints [27]. Closest to our setting, μSLAM [28] maintains a per-primitive confidence and plans on an occupancy grid projected from its Gaussian field. Its uncertainty never enters the planner, and the projected grid supports no planning beyond the ground plane. None of these systems, however, couples map uncer-tainty with the free-space reasoning required for real-time robot exploration. This motivates GOFusion ’s unified hybrid map representation that keeps Gaussian optimization local to the active submap while exposing a per-primitive uncertainty for downstream decisions. III. A. Experimental Setup 

We evaluate the proposed method on two public datasets and a real-world flight, comparing against state-of-the-art baselines throughout. TUM RGB-D [31] provides real-world indoor sequences with motion-capture ground-truth trajectories, while the uHumans2 [8] offers larger-scale synthetic environments with dense ground-truth geometry. The flight experiments additionally demonstrate real-time exploration of unknown, larger-scale environments, with the proposed Fisher informa-tion guiding view selection to refine the reconstruction. All experiments run on a workstation with an Intel Core Ultra 9-285K CPU, 96 GB of RAM, and an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU with 48 GB of VRAM, which also serves as the ground station for the outdoor flight 1. In the real-world experiment, we used a self-built quadcopter with a MicroStrain 3DM-GX5-25 IMU, a custom stereo rig of two ZED X One GS cameras, and a Jetson Orin NX with 16 GB of unified memory, running visual-inertial SLAM alongside a PX4 flight controller. In contrast to the public datasets, which provide low-resolution RGB and depth images at 640 × 480 , our outdoor-flight experiments use 1920 × 1200 images 2 at 15 Hz together with IMU measurements at 200 Hz . For DiskChunGS [23], we supply depth directly to bypass its stereo-depth network without loading or executing it. Unlike several baselines that evaluate only on valid-depth regions, we compute peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), struc-tural similarity index measure (SSIM) and learned perceptual image patch similarity (LPIPS) over the full image for all methods, since regions without depth returns are still rendered. This is why our reproduced scores for some baselines fall be-low the numbers reported in their papers. We further measure absolute trajectory error (ATE) RMSE, the number of Gaussian primitives, and peak GPU VRAM usage. Mapping frames per second (FPS) (Mapp. FPS) reports mapping throughput and full-system FPS (Full-FPS) the full tracking-and-mapping pipeline, both excluding the post-SLAM refinement.

C. Ablation Studies 1) Submapping and Joint Optimization: We study the submap-based map representation and joint Gaussian-pose optimization on uHumans2 [8], using only RGB supervision. As shown in Table IV, partitioning the map into submaps improves every rendering metric, slightly raises full-system FPS, and reduces peak memory, since only the active submap carries training buffers. Because each submap covers a small region, its local Gaussians stay consistent with the underlying geometry, where a global pose correction is applied to all of them at once. Besides, the joint optimization yields further gains across all rendering metrics at no cost in full-system FPS, and lowers the ATE of the single map but not of the submaps, whose poses are already well constrained by loop closure and submap alignment. 

2) Depth and Normal Supervision for 3DGS: We further investigate the impact of geometric supervision through depth and normals in (8). Fig. 4 illustrates exemplary RGB render-ings alongside their rendered normals from novel viewpoints on uHumans2 [8] 5. Accordingly, depth and normal supervi-sion consistently maintain surface geometry and substantially improve novel viewpoints, while the RGB renderings remain comparable. As discussed in [32], regularizing the 3DGS optimization toward geometric consistency prevents overfitting to the training-view appearance, which matters more for robot navigation than training-view rendering quality. However, this comes at the cost of a slight drop in rendering quality on the training views in our experiments, as shown in Table II. Consequently, the weights for depth and normal loss terms in (8) have to be carefully balanced.

C ONCLUSION 

We present GOFusion , a hybrid mapping method that anchors 3D Gaussians to local-occupancy submaps from visual-inertial SLAM to support real-time robot navigation. Beyond spatial awareness for path planning and high-fidelity appearance, it enables Fisher-guided Gaussian pruning and next-best-view selection. Across our extensive evaluations and a real-world autonomous flight, GOFusion achieves higher rendering quality than existing Gaussian SLAM systems with bounded GPU memory usage and successfully completes sequences that several baselines fail to complete. However, our method still assumes accurate and robust depth estimation, requiring careful tuning of the depth and normal supervision during 3DGS optimization. To maintain the computational efficiency on resource-constrained devices, optimizing one submap at a time limits the online rendering quality where neighboring submaps overlap or are misaligned, which would become more pronounced in semi-static or dynamic environments. Future work will incorporate depth uncertainty into the mapping objective, extend the optimization across neighboring submaps, and use semantic cues to handle dynamic environments. ACKNOWLEDGMENTS
