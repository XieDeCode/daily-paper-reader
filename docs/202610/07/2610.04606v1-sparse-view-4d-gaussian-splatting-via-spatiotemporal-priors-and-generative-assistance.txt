We present a 4D Gaussian Splatting framework for the Sparse-View Track of the SIGGRAPH Asia 2026 Volumetric Video Challenge , which requires dynamic scene reconstruction from only six cameras with wide baselines. To achieve robust dynamic reconstruction under such sparse views, our framework integrates three components. (1) Region-adaptive spatial pri-ors: We use foreground masks to guide Gaussian initialization and mask voting to control densification separately for the dynamic foreground and static background. Background geometry is regularized using monocular depth aligned to metric scale. (2) Motion-consistent temporal priors: 

We provide supervision at intermediate times through frame interpolation and constrain projected Gaussian motion with estimated optical flow. (3) Generative assistance: We place virtual cameras in the widest angular gaps and restore their rendered images using a diffusion-based model condi-tioned on camera poses. The restored images are iteratively incorporated into training as pseudo-supervision. On the validation set, our framework improves full-frame PSNR from 25.60 dB for the baseline to 29.75 dB. On the official test benchmark, it achieves 30.04 dB full-frame PSNR and 27.88 dB 

foreground PSNR, ranking first overall in the Sparse-View Track. CCS Concepts: • Computing methodologies → Image-based rendering ;

Volumetric models ; Reconstruction; Machine learning approaches. Additional Key Words and Phrases: volumetric video, 4D Gaussian splatting, sparse-view reconstruction, novel view synthesis

1 Introduction 

Dynamic 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) [Wang et al. 2025; Wu et al. 2024; Yang et al. 2026] enables real-time novel view synthesis of dynamic scenes, but typically assumes dense multi-view coverage. The Sparse-View Track of the SIGGRAPH Asia 2026 Volumetric Video Challenge challenges this reliance by requiring dynamic reconstruc-tion from only six wide-baseline cameras. Under such sparse-view settings, 4DGS reconstruction faces three interrelated challenges. (1) Imbalanced capacity allocation : Uni-form densification can overpopulate the background, which occu-pies most image pixels, while leaving the dynamic foreground un-derrepresented. This imbalance can lead to background overfitting and degrade foreground rendering at novel viewpoints. (2) Under-constrained motion : Rapid motion can produce blur and large inter-frame displacements, while residual timing differences across cameras cause nominally simultaneous images to capture different motion states. These factors limit the reliability of photometric su-pervision for continuous motion reconstruction. (3) Incomplete view coverage : Wide angular gaps and occlusions leave some re-gions weakly observed or unseen. Holes and incomplete structures may therefore persist despite improved capacity allocation and mo-tion regularization. To address these interrelated challenges, we combine three com-plementary components (Fig. 1): (1) Region-adaptive spatial pri-ors (Sec. 2) use foreground masks to guide initialization, mask voting to adapt densification, and monocular depth aligned to metric scale to regularize background geometry. (2) Motion-consistent tempo-ral priors (Sec. 3) provide supervision between captured frames through interpolation and constrain projected Gaussian velocities with estimated optical flow. (3) Generative assistance (Sec. 4) places virtual cameras in the widest angular gaps, restores their ren-dered images using ArtiFixer [de Lutio et al. 2026], and iteratively incorporates the restored views into training as pseudo-supervision. Extensive validation confirms that our framework effectively stabilizes sparse dynamic reconstruction, ultimately earning #1 

place in the official Sparse-View Track.

Given videos from six cameras with wide baselines, our framework combines three components: (1) region-adaptive spatial priors use YOLO11 and SAM 2 masks for per-frame foreground initialization and mask voting during densification, and Depth Anything V2 predictions for background depth regularization; (2) motion-consistent temporal priors use RIFE interpolation to provide supervision at intermediate times and VideoFlow estimates to constrain projected Gaussian motion; and (3) generative assistance restores images rendered from virtual cameras in wide angular gaps using the ArtiFixer diffusion model [de Lutio et al. 2026], with the nearest training views in camera pose space as references. The restored images are iteratively incorporated into training as pseudo-supervision, yielding the final 4DGS model for novel view synthesis.

3 Motion-Consistent Temporal Priors 

While spatial priors improve capacity allocation, photometric super-vision alone may still yield ambiguous Gaussian motion estimates, particularly in the presence of rapid motion and residual timing differences across cameras. We introduce two complementary tem-poral priors, both computed offline. Intermediate frames generated by RIFE [Huang et al. 2022] provide photometric supervision be-tween captured frames, while optical flow estimated by VideoFlow (BOFNet) [Shi et al. 2023] regularizes projected Gaussian motion.

4 Generative Assistance 

Despite spatial and temporal regularization, incomplete view cover-age due to wide angular gaps and occlusions can still lead to missing structures and floaters at novel viewpoints. To provide additional supervision at these viewpoints, we render images from virtual cameras and restore them using a diffusion model conditioned on camera poses and reference views. The restored images are itera-tively incorporated into 4DGS training as pseudo-supervision.

5 Implementation Details 

We train for 30 epochs at half resolution and evaluate at full resolu-tion. Each epoch visits every camera–time-slot pair once, following the sampling schedule in Sec. 3.1. We use spherical harmonics (SH) of degree 1 and set the near clipping distance to 0 .5. For region-adaptive spatial priors, we set 𝑉 tol = 3, 𝐾 bg = 10, initial 𝜏 fg /𝜏 bg = 0.01s /1.0s, 

𝜃 fg = 5×10 −6, 𝜃 bg = 1×10 −5, and 𝜆 depth = 1.0. For motion-consistent temporal priors, we set 𝐾 rife = 16, 𝐸 rife = 2, and 𝜆 flow = 1.0. For gen-erative assistance, we use 𝑁 virt = 14, 𝑁 round = 2, and 𝐸 tail = 2. 

6 Experiments and Analysis 

We evaluate on the official validation set, using six calibrated cam-eras for training and eight held-out cameras for novel view evalua-tion. Following the challenge protocol, we report PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS with an AlexNet backbone on both full frames and isolated dynamic foreground regions. All 4DGS models are trained on a single NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU.

Overcoming Incomplete View Coverage. As shown in Table 1 and Fig. 5, iterative generative assistance successfully completes under-observed regions—such as the mop tucked in the corner oc-cluded by the ladder and table—while substantially suppressing floaters, improving full-frame PSNR by +1.12 dB (to 29.75 dB) and improving perceptual LPIPS. Although SSIM slightly decreases due to subtle high-frequency variations introduced by diffusion comple-tion, the overall visual coherence and fidelity improve notably.
