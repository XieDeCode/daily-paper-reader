Abstract —Gaussian splatting has recently emerged as an ef-ficient representation for images and videos due to its explicit structure and fast rendering capability. Existing Gaussian-based video representations often decompose a video into canonical Gaussians and temporal deformation. However, when a video contains large global motion such as camera movement, the canonical representation may become misaligned with individual frames, increasing the burden on the temporal deformation model. In this paper, we propose an affine-atlas canonical Gaussian representation, which constructs canonical Gaussians in a larger affine-aligned atlas space. Frame-wise affine transforms absorb global motion before canonical Gaussian construction, reducing the gap between the canonical representation and target frames. Since the proposed method only modifies the canonical construction stage, it can be integrated into existing canonical-Gaussian-based methods with negligible additional parameter cost. Experiments show that our method improves reconstruction quality especially for sequences with large camera motion. 

Index Terms —Video representation, Gaussian splatting, canon-ical Gaussian, affine transform 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Video representation aims to express video data in a com-pact form while preserving reconstruction quality. Neural video representations encode videos into network parame-ters [1]–[6], but their decoding process requires repeated neural network inference. Recently, Gaussian splatting has attracted attention as an explicit and efficient representation due to its fast rasterization capability [7]. This idea has also been extended to image and video representation [8]–[17]. Compared with purely neural representations, Gaussian-based methods have explicit parameters and can be rendered effi-ciently, making them suitable for high-speed video decoding. Many recent Gaussian-based video representation methods adopt a canonical-Gaussian-based formulation, where canon-ical Gaussians are constructed for a short temporal segment, i.e., a group of pictures (GOP), and a temporal model predicts frame-dependent deformation. This design is effective because it separates shared spatial structure from temporal variation, reducing redundant per-frame Gaussian parameters. Therefore, the quality of the canonical Gaussians plays a central role in both reconstruction accuracy and representation efficiency. However, when a video contains camera motion or large rigid motion, frames in the same GOP may not be well aligned. The canonical Gaussians must then represent multiple shifted

A. Neural Video Representation 

Neural video representation methods encode videos into neural network parameters and reconstruct frames through network inference [1]–[6]. These methods demonstrate that videos can be represented compactly by exploiting spatial and temporal redundancy. However, decoding requires repeated neural network inference for each frame, which can become a bottleneck when high decoding speed is required. In contrast, Gaussian-based representations use explicit primitives and rasterization, which can provide faster rendering. 

B. Gaussian Splatting for Images and Videos 

Gaussian splatting represents visual content using Gaussian primitives and enables efficient rasterization [7]. This idea has been extended to compact 2D image representation [8]–[11], video representation [12]–[17] and 4D representation [18]– [24]. As illustrated in Fig. 1, Gaussian-based video representation methods can be categorized into three types according to how they model temporal variation. The first category explicitly parameterizes temporal changes of Gaussian attributes [12], [13], [21], which directly controls Gaussian motion but may require frame-dependent parameters or temporal coefficients. The second category represents videos using spatio-temporal Gaussian structures [25], [26], which directly model temporal continuity but can make representation and optimization more complex. The third category uses canonical Gaussians and temporal deformation [14]–[17], where shared spatial structure is represented by canonical Gaussians and temporal variation is modeled by a deformation field or network. This design is particularly attractive for video representation and compres-sion because it separates shared spatial content from temporal variation. 

C. Canonical-Gaussian-Based Video Representation 

Among Gaussian-based video representations, canonical-Gaussian-based methods are closely related to our work. D2GV [14] first fits 2D Gaussians to all frames in a GOP as a single canonical image and then learns temporal deformation using an MLP. GSVR [16] follows a similar strategy but uses a hybrid deformation field consisting of triplane grids and polynomial motion. STGV [17] introduces spatio-temporal hash encoding for efficient temporal modeling. These methods demonstrate the effectiveness of the canon-ical Gaussian plus deformation framework. However, when frames in a GOP are not well aligned due to global motion, the canonical space can become suboptimal, increasing the burden on temporal deformation. Therefore, how to construct canonical Gaussians is a key factor for achieving efficient and accurate video representation. This motivates our affine-atlas formulation, which improves the canonical construction stage by absorbing global motion before temporal deformation. III. P ROPOSED METHOD 

A. Overview 

We propose an affine-atlas canonical Gaussian representa-tion for efficient video representation. As shown in Fig. 2, the proposed method constructs canonical Gaussians in a larger atlas space instead of directly optimizing them in the original frame coordinate space. Each frame in a GOP is associated with a frame-wise 6-parameter affine transform, and the frames are aligned in the atlas space before canonical Gaussian construction. This design allows global motion, such as translation, rotation, and scaling, to be absorbed before canonical Gaussian construction. After this construction stage, each baseline temporal deformation model is applied without modification.

A. Experimental Setup 

We conduct experiments on Big Buck Bunny [27] and the UVG dataset [28]. For Big Buck Bunny, we use 132 frames with a resolution of 1280 × 720 . For the UVG dataset, we use a spatial resolution of 1920 × 1080 and temporally subsample each sequence by a factor of five. Each video sequence is divided into GOPs of 10 frames, and each GOP is reconstructed independently. This setting follows the common video representation pipeline, where canonical Gaussians and temporal deformation are optimized for each GOP. We evalu-ate the reconstruction quality using PSNR and MS-SSIM. We compare each baseline method with its affine-atlas variant: D2GV, GSVR, and STGV are evaluated with and without the proposed canonical Gaussian construction. In all variants, only the canonical construction stage is replaced, while the temporal modeling component of each method is kept unchanged. For the affine-atlas variants, frame-wise affine transforms are precomputed using ORB feature matching. We estimate a two-dimensional affine transform between adjacent frames and compose them to obtain frame-wise transforms within each GOP. The estimated affine matrices are fixed during training and are not optimized as learnable parameters. We also use the average translation magnitude of the adjacent-frame affine transforms as the global motion magnitude for sorting UVG sequences in Table I. 

B. Results and Analysis 

Table I shows the quantitative comparison on Big Buck Bunny and the UVG dataset. The proposed affine-atlas con-struction improves reconstruction quality, especially for se-quences with large camera motion, such as Bosphorus, Jockey, and ReadySetGo. This tendency is observed across the eval-uated baseline methods, suggesting that the proposed method improves the canonical Gaussian construction stage rather than a specific temporal deformation model. C. Parameter Cost 

The proposed method introduces only frame-wise affine transform matrices and does not add a neural network or mod-ify the temporal deformation model. Since a two-dimensional affine transform is represented by a 2×3 matrix, the additional cost is only 6T parameters for a GOP with T frames. For example, a 10-frame GOP requires only 60 affine parameters, which is negligible compared with the Gaussian and temporal-model parameters, typically on the order of hundreds of thou-sands per GOP. This suggests that the performance gain comes from better canonical alignment, not from simply increasing the model size. V. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, we propose an affine-aligned atlas for canon-ical Gaussian construction in video representation. By con-structing canonical Gaussians in a larger affine-aligned atlas space, the proposed method absorbs global motion before temporal deformation learning and reduces the gap between the canonical representation and target frames. Experiments on Big Buck Bunny and UVG sequences show that our method improves reconstruction quality, especially for sequences with large camera motion, with negligible parameter cost.
