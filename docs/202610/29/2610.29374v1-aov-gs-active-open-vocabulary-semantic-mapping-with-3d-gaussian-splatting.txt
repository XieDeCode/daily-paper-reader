Abstract. Robots deployed in human-centered environments need maps that support both geometric navigation and open-vocabulary se-mantic queries beyond a fixed label set. A key challenge is enriching such maps with semantic information without relying on manually annotated segmentation masks or closed-set semantic categories. Existing geometry-driven active mapping maximizes coverage but treats semantics as a passive by-product of the collected trajectory, whereas active semantic mapping typically depends on supervised semantic segmentation. We present AOV-GS , an active 3D Gaussian mapping system that guides exploration using frozen foundation models. During coarse exploration, AOV-GS re-ranks geometrically promising candidate views according to CLIP mask-embedding novelty computed from SAM instance segmenta-tion, biasing exploration toward semantically informative regions. Frozen SAM and CLIP provide instance masks and language-aligned embed-dings throughout mapping, while a lightweight scene-specific language field is fitted offline directly to the collected CLIP embeddings rather than manually annotated masks, enabling open-vocabulary text-query segmentation on novel views. In a controlled comparison using the same SAM–CLIP perception stack, AOV-GS improves average language-field mIoU over geometry-only active planning by 1.3 percentage points on Replica (34.6% on eight scenes) and by 1.8 points on Matterport3D (23.1% on five scenes). With CorrCLIP, AOV-GS reaches 35.6% on Replica, 2.0 points above the matched Geometry-based + CorrCLIP baseline. Novel-view PSNR remains comparable to the geometry-only baseline on both datasets while depth L1 improves on Replica. Code is available at github.com/OptimusFaber/AOV-GS.

## 1 Introduction 

Mobile robots operating in human-centered environments need spatial maps of the environment that support both navigation and task-level semantic interac-tion. In practice this means a map should render high-quality RGB-D views 

and support queries such as find the chair or locate the table near the win-dow . These objectives are coupled through exploration: the viewpoints collected arXiv:2610.29374v1 [cs.CV] 21 Oct 2026 2 R. Shkokov et al. 

during mapping determine geometric completeness, object visibility, and the ev-idence available for downstream open-vocabulary retrieval. Recent 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) SLAM systems [13, 11] achieve ef-ficient high-fidelity reconstruction suitable for online robotic mapping. Active planners such as ActiveGS [10], ActiveSplat [19], and ActiveGAMER [3] im-prove coverage through geometry-aware next-best-view (NBV) selection. In par-allel, foundation-model perception enables open-vocabulary semantics: Segment Anything (SAM) [15] provides prompt-free instance masks and CLIP [24] maps mask regions to a language-aligned embedding space. LangSplat [23] and related methods attach such features to 3D primitives for text-query segmentation, but typically assume a fixed observation trajectory. Active semantic mapping closes part of this gap. ActiveSGM [4] demon-strates that semantic signals can guide exploration in a 3DGS loop, but relies on per-scene OneFormer [7] fine-tuning, which limits open-vocabulary deployment. Passive fusion pipelines [8, 22] can improve semantic quality on pre-recorded trajectories but do not affect online data acquisition. This paper addresses the following question: Can a robot actively explore an unseen scene using frozen foundation models, improve open-vocabulary segmenta-tion through viewpoint selection, and preserve competitive RGB-D reconstruction quality? 

We answer affirmatively with AOV-GS (Fig. 1). During coarse exploration (stage 0), geometric information gain is augmented with CLIP-embedding nov-elty computed from SAM instance masks on RGB views rendered from the cur-rent Gaussian map. Semantic novelty is measured against a growing keyframe embedding bank and re-ranks the top-K geometrically strong candidates. Dur-ing refinement (stage 1+), planning reverts to geometry-only behavior as in ActiveGS [10]. After exploration, per-keyframe CLIP mask embeddings train a sparse language field on Gaussian primitives, enabling open-vocabulary text-query segmentation on novel views. As in ActiveSGM, candidate RGB views for semantic scoring are rendered from the current Gaussian map; Habitat supplies the posed RGB-D observation after the selected view is executed. The experi-ments nevertheless use ground-truth camera poses as oracle SLAM input. Our contributions are: 

– A coarse exploration NBV criterion that re-ranks the top-K candidates pro-duced by the geometric planner according to CLIP mask-embedding novelty, steering the robot toward semantically novel object instances while reverting to geometry-only scoring during later refinement stages. 

– An active open-vocabulary semantic mapping pipeline combining online se-mantic exploration with offline LangSplatV2 language-field construction, without per-scene segmenter fine-tuning. 

– A two-benchmark evaluation showing average open-vocabulary gains over geometry-only exploration while preserving comparable novel-view recon-struction quality. ## 2 Related Work 

Active perception for neural mapping uses information gain and coverage criteria to select informative views [9, 6, 21]. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [13, 11] lowers planning and rendering cost relative to Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [20], enabling online systems such as ActiveGS [10], ActiveSplat [19], and ActiveGAMER [3]; these methods optimize geometry but leave semantic map utility to the collected trajectory. Semantic and open-vocabulary Gaussian mapping [17, 26, 23, 14, 1, 34] attaches language-aligned features to 3D primitives for text queries, typically on fixed trajectories; our language-field head follows LangSplatV2 [18], but AOV-GS uses semantic novelty to choose views during exploration rather than only processing a passive path. Recent semantic map-ping systems also include LEG-SLAM [28] and M3DMap [30], which integrate language-aligned or object-aware semantics into 3D reconstruction. Closely related but complementary is SegmATRon [31], an embodied active segmen-tation method that generates agent actions to maximize image segmentation quality rather than building a persistent semantic map. ActiveSGM [4] couples geometric and semantic terms during 3DGS exploration but requires per-scene OneFormer fine-tuning [7]. Passive open-vocabulary fusion [8, 22] improves semantics on recorded paths without steering acquisition. AOV-GS trades closed-set semantic supervision for open-vocabulary scene representations based on frozen SAM and CLIP.

3.1 Gaussian Mapping Backbone 

We adopt SplaTAM [11] as the reconstruction backbone with ActiveGS-style active planning infrastructure [10]. Habitat [25] provides synchronized RGB-D frames with ground-truth camera poses. In both Replica and Matterport3D ex-periments, these poses are used as oracle SLAM input (Sec. 4.1). The scene is represented by isotropic 3D Gaussians optimized through differentiable ren-dering with RGB and depth L1 losses over silhouette pixels [11, 10]. Follow-ing ActiveGS [10], we maintain a voxel-based exploration map for collision-free candidate pose sampling and execute selected views with an RRT-based local planner [16].

3.4 Language Field Construction and Query 

After exploration, stored CLIP mask embeddings train a LangSplatV2 sparse language field on the reconstructed Gaussians. A global codebook is initialized by k-means over mask embeddings; each Gaussian receives sparse coefficients over codebook entries. At inference, a text prompt is encoded by CLIP and matched against multi-scale rendered cosine-similarity maps ( s/m/l ), producing an open-vocabulary segmentation mask on a novel view (Fig. 4). 4.2 Open-Vocabulary Semantic Segmentation on Replica 

Table 1 summarizes open-vocabulary language-field mIoU on eight Replica scenes. AOV-GS reaches 34.6% average mIoU without CorrCLIP (+1.3 over Geometry-based, +2.5 over Passive). With CorrCLIP, AOV-GS reaches 35.6%, compared with 33.6% for Geometry-based + CorrCLIP. ActiveSGM (78.8% closed-set mIoU in our runs) uses per-scene OneFormer on fixed label sets and is listed only as a supervised reference outside our open-vocabulary protocol. To better understand the heterogeneous per-scene results, we analyzed scene size, object statistics, lighting conditions, and semantic evaluation subsets. No consistent correlation was observed with scene area or object count alone. In-stead, improvements were primarily determined by the reliability of semantic observations. Large, well-segmented objects produced stable SAM masks and CLIP embeddings, whereas adverse visual conditions (including low illumina-tion, overexposure, and cluttered scenes containing many small objects) reduced semantic feature quality and limited the benefit of semantic planning. The Cor-rCLIP improvement on office2 (+8.6 pp) suggests that semantic feature quality is an important bottleneck. CorrCLIP is applied both to candidate masks used for semantic re-ranking and to accepted keyframe masks used to populate the embedding bank, so this result combines planner and representation effects. 4.3 RGB-D Reconstruction on Replica 

A practical semantic planner should preserve reconstruction quality while im-proving semantic coverage. Table 3 reports eight-scene averages from a single run per method. AOV-GS matches the Geometry-based planner on PSNR (31.19 vs. 31.47 dB), SSIM, and LPIPS, while improving depth L1 (1.18 vs. 1.26 cm). To assess stability, we repeat Geometry-based and AOV-GS on all eight scenes with five independent seeds (0–4). Table 4 summarizes pooled render-ing metrics and office0 language-field mIoU. Cross-seed PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS have similar descriptive means and spreads. On office0 , semantic mIoU is 33 .6±1.1% for AOV-GS versus 28 .8±1.4% for Geometry-based. 

4.4 Computational Cost 

Stage-0 semantic scoring applies only to top-K candidates ( ≈8 s per view on 

office0 on an NVIDIA RTX 4070 Ti SUPER); stage 1+ adds no semantic overhead. A complete office0 run takes 52:32, 61:23, and 70:41 for K=4, 8, and 16, respectively, with peak GPU memory of 12.59, 12.92, and 13.80 GB. Because candidate views are scored sequentially, increasing K affects runtime more strongly than memory. Offline language-field training peaks at 12.6 GB with the default 0.5 downscale and ranges from 9.9 to 29.1 GB across the evaluated render-downscale settings; runtime increases with the number of rendered pixels. Multi-scale text-query decoding takes 1.41 s per query–view pair and peaks at 4.2 GB. On Matterport3D, both open-vocabulary planners fit in 16 GB on this hardware, whereas ActiveSGM in our setup required ≈36–48 GB due to the OneFormer module.

## 5 Conclusion 

We presented AOV-GS, an active open-vocabulary semantic mapping pipeline that guides exploration with CLIP mask-embedding novelty from frozen SAM and CLIP and trains a scene-specific LangSplatV2 language field on the collected embeddings for text-query segmentation on novel views. Across Replica and Mat-terport3D, the resulting maps improve average open-vocabulary language-field mIoU over geometry-only exploration while preserving comparable novel-view reconstruction quality. Replica gains vary by scene, while the five-seed office0 

experiment shows a consistent advantage over geometry-only exploration. Experiments further show that these gains depend on alignment between ex-ploration and the downstream semantic representation: trajectories optimized for AOV-GS 15 

a closed-set OneFormer pipeline do not transfer to a SAM–CLIP language field, whereas semantic novelty co-designed with that representation improves the av-erage on both benchmarks. The evaluation uses ground-truth camera poses, matching the simulator protocol used by the compared active-mapping meth-ods; robustness to SLAM drift and real sensor noise is outside the scope of this simulator study.
